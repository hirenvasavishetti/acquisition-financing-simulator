# Financial methodology — current public and synthetic cases

All amounts are USD millions; share counts are millions unless source units say otherwise. Original fiscal periods remain attached to historical observations. Forecast years are five full post-close model years from 1 November 2025, not company fiscal years.

## Timing and debt service

`frequency=1` settles annually; `frequency=4` settles quarterly. The selected frequency enters every capacity and reverse-stress run. The synthetic report also presents both capacity results under the same operating assumptions. Public mode stays annual because verified quarterly history is insufficient to seed trailing liquidity/coverage tests.

Calendar period ends are generated by month arithmetic. A debt instrument with an ISO maturity date pays in the period containing that date; otherwise `maturity` is elapsed model years from closing. DKS retained notes carry sourced 15 January 2032 and 2052 dates. The aggregate finance-lease schedule takes precedence over its sentinel maturity. Synthetic benchmarks intentionally use documented relative-year maturities.

Interest uses opening principal for the whole period. Period-end draws accrue from the next period; intra-period principal changes are not daily prorated. Mandatory principal precedes optional sweeps. Due unpaid principal remains debt with an explicit funding gap. Refinancing rolls only eligible due principal, within an assumed cumulative limit, and creates a new priced instrument. It is not committed liquidity. Quarterly timing can either increase or decrease capacity through interest, amortisation, seasonal working capital and sweeps; do not force equality with annual results.

Cash floor, net leverage and cash-interest coverage are analytical screens, never legal covenants. Every period and closing are tested. Nonpositive EBITDA makes ratio screens unavailable and fails them; zero interest with positive EBITDA has no coverage breach. Negative cash is an unfunded deficit. Projection continues mechanically after breaches to quantify needs, not to represent an executable path.

## Accounting costs versus forecast behaviour

DKS FY2024: revenue 13,442.849 less reported cost of sales 8,617.153, SG&A 3,294.272 and preopening 57.492 gives operating profit 1,473.932. Add disclosed D&A 400.409: starting EBITDA 1,874.341.

FL FY2024: total revenue 7,988 less cost of sales 5,666, SG&A 1,920, separately presented D&A 202 and impairment/other 97 gives operating profit 103. Add back only D&A 202: starting EBITDA 305. The 97 is not silently normalised away. Foot Locker revenue includes licensing revenue; D&A presentation differs from DKS.

Accounting COS includes occupancy and other partly fixed costs. SG&A is not necessarily entirely fixed. Subtract separately modelled lease expense (782.92 / 931) and starting EBITDA from revenue to obtain nonrent cost pools 10,785.588 / 6,752. Base assumptions allocate 80% of these pools to variable costs. Alternatives use 65% and 95%, recalibrating fixed costs to preserve identical starting EBITDA. These percentages are exploratory choices, not empirical estimates. The engine's public `cogs` is aggregate model-variable cost, not reported COS. Working-capital calibration independently uses reported COS, preventing cost-behaviour edits from changing inventory/payables mechanically.

Revenue follows explicit five-year growth arrays. Fixed costs/rent grow 2% by default; cash rent differs from rent expense only by the disclosed calibration factor. EBITDA already deducts rent; operating cash adjusts the cash/expense difference only. D&A and capex are revenue ratios. Simplified cash tax is 27% of positive EBIT less permitted model interest, with no loss carryforwards or jurisdiction detail. Synergies and one-off integration cash are separate.

## Connected closing bridge

For each company, start with verified FY2024 cash and inventory/receivables/payables. Base closing multipliers are inventory 1.18/1.15, receivables 1.05/1.00 and payables 1.15/1.15. These are future scenarios, not historical facts or fitted seasonal estimates. High/low closing-WC scenarios vary inventory by ±10 percentage points and payables in the opposite direction.

Remove separately disclosed trade-WC cash contributions from prior 39-week CFO, apply a prospective CFO factor, then deduct projected change in trade NWC **once**. DKS: (680.307 - (-497.485)) × 1 - 389.02045 = 788.77155 projected CFO. Pre-close capex = initial annual budget × prior 39-week/full-year capex ratio. Deduct projected dividends, accrued buybacks and finance-lease principal to reach projected closing cash. Post-close NWC changes start from these same projected balances.

FL's separately disclosed inventory/payable contribution is -106; its AR contribution is not separate. Hold projected FL AR constant and leave other operating cash as a proxy. Other assets/liabilities, taxes, stock compensation, FX, acquisitions and equity issuance are not a full balance-sheet forecast. Prior-year seasonality is only a calibration reference and may not repeat.

Accounting cash differs from accessible cash. Base assumes 75% of projected DKS cash accessible; no FL opening cash funds the deal. Scenarios test 0–100% accessibility and positive operating-cash accessibility separately, retaining all negative flows. Reserve is retained from accessible cash before cash-first funding. Excluded cash is never spent. Projected negative closing cash becomes an additional closing use. No haircut proves legal availability.

## Uses, fees and analytical equity

Uses separately show cash consideration, award reserves, principal redeemed, premium, accrued interest, transaction expenses, financing fees, LC reserve and other uses. Public FL notes are redeemed at the sourced call schedule (projected 101% plus one month accrued at the illustrative closing date); DKS notes remain outstanding. The FL ABL terminates. No existing facility is counted as post-close liquidity. A 105% LC cash reserve is an assumption, not a release quote.

Mixed consideration assumes 50% common-share stock election at the published exchange ratio. Converted employee awards and withheld taxes are not asserted to be fully modelled; common-share dilution is not fully diluted ownership. Analytical equity in a capacity test is separate funding, not additional transaction stock election.

For actual-issued-debt fees, let U be fixed nonfinancing uses, C chosen cash contribution, A0 originally eligible acquisition borrowing and A tested acquisition borrowing. F = U - C - A0. Issued debt D = (A + F)/(1 - fee rate), fees = D × rate, analytical equity = A0 - A. Then C + D + equity equals U + fees. There is no unexplained balancing plug. Fixed-budget mode retains the original fee and other funding budget while substituting equity for A0-A. Public defaults to issued-debt fees; the original synthetic comparison defaults to fixed-budget fees for reproducibility.

## Reinvestment and uncertainty

Initial gross capex budgets are 1,200/300; assumed maintenance allocations 800/240 leave residual growth budgets 400/60. These are analyst allocations, not disclosed minimum maintenance. Mechanical lower-investment cases use 900/240 while keeping operations fixed. Linked cases additionally reduce annual growth by 1 or 2 percentage points; no precise capex-to-growth elasticity is inferred. Dividends and synergy delivery are independently varied. A maintenance assumption exceeding gross budget is capped for presentation; this is not proof that operations can be sustained.

## Capacity, stress and evidence

Capacity scans the eligible acquisition-borrowing interval including zero and the funding endpoint. It refines every sampled feasibility transition without assuming global monotonicity: synthetic grid 10/refinement 1; public 25/5. Narrow feasible islands between samples may be missed. No feasible point means the complete operating/funding policy fails the model screens even at zero acquisition borrowing; it is not a claim of zero standalone lending capacity. Reverse revenue stress scans until the first tested breach, reporting an already failing base and the step size.

Source checks require pre-cutoff publication, document integrity, exact-value claims, compatible units/periods and nonconflicting observations. Hashes prove integrity, not factual accuracy. Code-owned derivations retain source dependencies. Facts never become assumptions just to bypass a failure. Every output declares its own dependencies; a missing debt contract can block financing while leaving the cash bridge and historical arithmetic available. The strict 109-input policy and materiality reasons remain visible. Unbounded private lease consent, withholding and exceptional commitments prevent an overall actual-company preference.

Forecast assumptions are in `financing/public_defaults.json`; editable example: `data/public-assumptions.json`. Scenario variations are explicit in `scenario_definitions`. The live endpoint accepts an allow-list of future choices; historical facts cannot be changed through it. Assumption, source, case, configuration and Python-code fingerprints accompany saved reports. SQLite retains observations, reviewed evidence, runs and scenario payloads. HTML, workbook and memo consume those same results; no separate financial model runs in JavaScript or Excel.
